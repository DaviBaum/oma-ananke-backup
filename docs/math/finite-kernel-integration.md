# Finite optimization kernel integration

The callable kernels implement explicit finite models. They preserve unresolved alternatives and bind their conclusions to immutable inputs. Their arithmetic checks do not establish physical validity of an IFC materialization or certify a complete continuous route universe.

| Mechanism | Callable implementation | Independent replay | Input contract |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contextual quotient | `oma.optimization.finite.contextual_quotient` | `verify_contextual_quotient` | Every state has a value for every declared experiment; the experiment family and context root are explicit. |
| Finite archive optimum | `finite_archive_optimum` | Exhaustive policy/co-design replays below | PASS/FAIL outcomes carry upstream evidence roots. UNKNOWN alternatives retain exact costs or valid lower bounds when available. A complete archive is not enough when an unresolved alternative could improve the incumbent. |
| Chance constraint | `finite_chance_constraint` | Exact rational interval arithmetic | One complete finite joint probability law summing to one; missing event truth is UNKNOWN. No independent-event assumption is introduced. |
| Expected loss, worst listed loss, CVaR | `finite_risk` | Finite CVaR breakpoint evaluation | Losses and probabilities are exact rational inputs. A finite scenario law does not prove a universal uncertainty guarantee. |
| Quantity split/merge | `check_quantity_transport` | Exact row and column residuals | Nonnegative allocations preserve each source and target quantity in the same unit. This does not transport geometric, pressure, stiffness, or certificate semantics. |
| Nonanticipative policy | `oma.optimization.policy.solve_finite_policy` | `verify_finite_policy_result` | A decision belongs to an information-history node. All scenarios with the same history use that decision. Leaf outcomes are supplied by domain checkers. |
| Strategic co-design | `oma.optimization.codesign.solve_finite_codesign` | `verify_finite_codesign_result` | Every design case carries its assignment, materialization evidence, exact candidate root, capital cost, and a routing master rooted in that same candidate. |

`FinitePolicyProblem` takes `Scenario(id, history, probability)` records, domains of `(stage, history_prefix, action_ids)`, and `LeafOutcome(scenario_id, actions, FiniteOutcome(...))` records. Each history token is observed before its corresponding action. A missing leaf becomes UNKNOWN. The objective is `EXPECTED_COST` or `WORST_LISTED_COST`; supplied leaf outcomes must already account for applicable capacity, geometry, physics, state transitions, and action costs. Decisions made before scenarios separate therefore share capacity and equipment choices. Neither solver nor checker silently solves each scenario clairvoyantly.

`FiniteCoDesignProblem` takes `DesignCase` records, explicit finite variable domains, protected assignment values, and a context root. Setting `declared_design_universe_complete=True` requires a case for every assignment, including failed or unresolved materializations. Multiple cases for one assignment are permitted, but that finite list must itself be the declared complete materialization universe before a broader completeness claim is made. The protected values checked here are assignment tokens; the BIM adapter must separately prove that protected source state remained unchanged. Capital and routing costs must share the objective's declared units, baseline, and sign convention.

The application adapter must retain each materialization and physical checker report as an immutable artifact, include their state and checker roots in the problem context, and submit only checked physical route columns to the master. It must preserve rejected and unresolved proposals in the search ledger. A rejected proposal is evidence against that proposal; claiming the corresponding entire design infeasible additionally requires complete routing/materialization coverage. Reuse of an unchanged column under a changed shaft or equipment position needs exact candidate-root evidence or a separately checked transport theorem.

Budget or cancellation stops return the incumbent when one exists and retain unresolved cases. Independent checkers also have explicit finite enumeration budgets; exceeding their budget is UNKNOWN. Optimality can close with unresolved candidates only when every one has a valid objective floor proving that it cannot improve the accepted incumbent. Such floors are input claims relative to the supplied table and need upstream evidence before the application treats them as physical bounds. Restricted routing masters provide no bound for omitted routes.

Amendment OMA-MATH-A007 records why a sound three-valued checker alone does not prove a finite global optimum. The source does not provide the missing universal materializers or physical dynamics, so these modules are named and scoped as finite kernels, not full PCJCD, PCDYN, or PCFAR packages.

The executable examples are in `tests/test_optimization_finite.py`, `tests/test_optimization_policy.py`, and `tests/test_optimization_codesign.py`. They include conflicting shared capacity, a nonanticipativity counterexample, unknown leaves, incomplete route domains, protected assignment rejection, stale roots, forged results, exact transport residuals, and independent checker budget failures.
