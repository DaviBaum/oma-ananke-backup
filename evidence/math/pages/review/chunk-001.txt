[1-10:P104; UTF16 7034] 4. Ten or More Radical Candidate Paradigms
[1-10:P105; UTF16 7077] 4.1 Generative-program supercompilation
[1-10:P106; UTF16 7117] Premise
[1-10:P107; UTF16 7125] Represent a building as a typed generative program. Search programs rather than explicit geometry. Use partial evaluation, common-subexpression elimination, recursion, macros, and program transformation to compress repeated floors, rooms, structural bays, and systems.
[1-10:P108; UTF16 7394] Source of leverage
[1-10:P109; UTF16 7413] A program can describe a trillion geometric instances in a few kilobytes. Repetition becomes a loop rather than duplicated objects. Construction logic, geometry, and physics generation can share parameters.
[1-10:P110; UTF16 7620] What kills it
[1-10:P111; UTF16 7634] Program-space search is still search over realizations. Two very different programs may have indistinguishable engineering consequences, while two nearly identical programs may cross a critical equipment or code threshold.
[1-10:P112; UTF16 7857] Semantic equivalence of arbitrary programs is generally too difficult. A program representation also risks preserving irrelevant implementation distinctions merely because they have different syntax.
[1-10:P113; UTF16 8057] Salvage
[1-10:P114; UTF16 8065] Use a typed generative language as ANANKE’s source language, but never treat programs themselves as the final semantic identity.
[1-10:P118; UTF16 8197] 4.2 Constructive proof-first synthesis
[1-10:P120; UTF16 8244] Treat a building as a constructive proof that an artifact satisfying a specification exists. Geometry, products, and details are witnesses extracted from the proof.
[1-10:P122; UTF16 8428] Logical propagation can eliminate impossible families without simulation. Every refinement preserves obligations. Release evidence is native rather than attached later.
[1-10:P124; UTF16 8611] Buildings are not defined entirely by theorem-like specifications. Uncertain futures, model discrepancy, costs, incomplete code interpretations, and architectural judgment cannot all be reduced honestly to propositions.
[1-10:P125; UTF16 8831] Moreover, proof search can simply move the combinatorial explosion into the space of lemmas and witnesses.
[1-10:P127; UTF16 8946] Make every elimination and transformation evidence-bearing, but allow multiple evidence types rather than pretending everything is mathematically proved.
[1-10:P131; UTF16 9103] 4.3 Abstract physical interpretation with CEGAR
[1-10:P133; UTF16 9159] Interpret enormous families of buildings through conservative abstract states:
[1-10:P134; UTF16 9238] interval geometry;
[1-10:P135; UTF16 9257] zonotopic loads;
[1-10:P136; UTF16 9274] symbolic routes;
[1-10:P137; UTF16 9291] capacity envelopes;
[1-10:P138; UTF16 9311] abstract product classes;
[1-10:P139; UTF16 9337] uncertain physics ranges.
[1-10:P140; UTF16 9363] When an abstraction is too coarse, a counterexample causes refinement.
[1-10:P142; UTF16 9453] One abstract state can conservatively represent millions of realizations. If its optimistic bound is infeasible or dominated, the whole family disappears.
[1-10:P143; UTF16 9608] This extends the logic of abstract interpretation and counterexample-guided abstraction refinement into engineering. ACM Digital Library
[1-10:P145; UTF16 9759] A fixed library of abstract domains becomes the bottleneck. If the abstraction does not match the project’s actual causal structure, intervals become uselessly broad and CEGAR degenerates toward concrete enumeration.
[1-10:P147; UTF16 9984] Sound set semantics and counterexample-driven splitting are essential, but the machine must also invent project-specific abstractions.
[1-10:P151; UTF16 10122] 4.4 Equality-saturated engineering
[1-10:P153; UTF16 10165] Place every equivalent formulation of a building subsystem into an engineering e-graph:
[1-10:P154; UTF16 10253] algebraically equivalent structural models;
[1-10:P155; UTF16 10297] interchangeable network representations;
[1-10:P156; UTF16 10338] route rewrites;
[1-10:P157; UTF16 10354] equivalent construction decompositions;
[1-10:P158; UTF16 10394] geometry transformations;
[1-10:P159; UTF16 10420] alternative solver formulations.
[1-10:P160; UTF16 10453] Apply rewrites without committing prematurely to one sequence.
[1-10:P162; UTF16 10535] Equality saturation avoids the phase-ordering problem in which one early transformation prevents a better later transformation. E-graphs are designed to represent many equivalent expressions through shared equivalence classes. arXiv
[1-10:P164; UTF16 10782] Ordinary equality is too strong in some places and too weak in others.
[1-10:P165; UTF16 10853] Two systems need not be mathematically identical to be interchangeable for the current decision. Conversely, approximate physical equivalence may fail under an unseen context. Uncontrolled e-graph saturation can also explode in memory.
[1-10:P167; UTF16 11097] Use bounded, contextual saturation inside small regions of ANANKE’s quotient circuit. Do not make one global e-graph the whole building brain.
[1-10:P171; UTF16 11243] 4.5 Knowledge-compiled hybrid decision circuits
[1-10:P173; UTF16 11299] Compile logical, discrete, probabilistic, and optimization structure into a circuit supporting rapid repeated queries.
[1-10:P174; UTF16 11418] Variables are eliminated. Repeated subproblems are shared. Queries such as feasibility, counting, best completion, and conditional choice become circuit evaluations.
[1-10:P176; UTF16 11603] Knowledge compilation can make repeated inference cheap after an expensive compilation. Variable elimination reveals that raw variable count is often less important than the width of the dependency structure. arXiv
[1-10:P178; UTF16 11832] Conventional tractable circuits are not designed for geometry-changing PDEs, model discrepancy, continuous high-dimensional fields, or human architectural intent. High dependency width can make compilation explode.
[1-10:P180; UTF16 12055] Use a hybrid circuit as the backbone, but invent new node semantics for physical operators, evidence, contextual equivalence, and continuous abstract domains.
[1-10:P184; UTF16 12217] 4.6 The port-operator universe
[1-10:P186; UTF16 12256] Never repeatedly simulate interiors. Represent every subsystem by its boundary behavior:
[1-10:P187; UTF16 12345] \[ \text{boundary conditions} \longmapsto \text{boundary responses and internal claims}. \]
[1-10:P188; UTF16 12437] A structural bay exposes stiffness and failure envelopes. A room exposes thermal, acoustic, airflow, lighting, occupancy, and service responses. A shaft exposes flow capacity, fire behavior, access, and routing constraints.
[1-10:P190; UTF16 12680] Local systems can be condensed, reduced, and reused. Repeated floors and rooms reuse the same operators. Global analysis becomes interface composition rather than full reconstruction.
[1-10:P191; UTF16 12864] Certified port reduction and static condensation already demonstrate that interface spaces can sometimes converge rapidly, while compositional open-system formalisms provide a mathematical language for gluing systems along boundaries. arXiv
[1-10:P193; UTF16 13119] Not every behavior is local or low-dimensional. Fire, wind, progressive collapse, smoke, turbulent flow, controls, and human movement can produce long-range nonlinear dependencies. An operator library alone also does not solve topology, intent, or decision allocation.
[1-10:P195; UTF16 13396] Proof-carrying port envelopes become ANANKE’s numerical semantics, with forced escalation when an interface representation loses validity.
[1-10:P199; UTF16 13538] 4.7 Causal-intervention synthesis

[1-10:P204; UTF16 13733] break a single-point failure domain;
[1-10:P205; UTF16 13770] relocate a high-load subsystem across a structural separator;
[1-10:P206; UTF16 13832] create a service-access corridor;
[1-10:P207; UTF16 13866] synchronize product thresholds;
[1-10:P208; UTF16 13898] decouple construction packages.
[1-10:P209; UTF16 13930] Thousands of geometric witnesses may implement one intervention.
[1-10:P211; UTF16 14014] The effective causal dimension can be far lower than the geometric dimension. One intervention can explain large cross-disciplinary effects.
[1-10:P213; UTF16 14169] The correct causal variables are not given. They are context dependent and may emerge only after detailed analysis. A hand-designed causal ontology would encode current human assumptions and could exclude novel mechanisms.
[1-10:P215; UTF16 14400] ANANKE must discover and validate causal sufficient statistics automatically.
[1-10:P219; UTF16 14481] 4.8 Hierarchical renormalization
[1-10:P221; UTF16 14522] Replace microscopic detail with effective theories at progressively larger scales:
[1-10:P222; UTF16 14605] fitting and connection;
[1-10:P223; UTF16 14629] service rack;
[1-10:P224; UTF16 14643] room;
[1-10:P225; UTF16 14649] bay;
[1-10:P226; UTF16 14654] floor;
[1-10:P227; UTF16 14661] vertical stack;
[1-10:P228; UTF16 14677] building;
[1-10:P229; UTF16 14687] site.
[1-10:P230; UTF16 14693] Each scale transmits only effective quantities needed by the next.
[1-10:P232; UTF16 14779] Repeated local detail vanishes into compact large-scale laws. A thousand fixtures may become a small hydraulic response; hundreds of façade panels may become effective thermal, moisture, and structural operators.
[1-10:P234; UTF16 15006] There is no universal building renormalization operator. Discrete product thresholds, egress rules, access constraints, rare failures, and architectural exceptions can prevent clean scale separation.
[1-10:P236; UTF16 15214] Use project-specific coarse-graining and retain explicit exception channels. Never assume universal low-rank behavior.
[1-10:P240; UTF16 15336] 4.9 Tensorized design families
[1-10:P242; UTF16 15375] Represent enormous high-dimensional response functions using tensor trains, hierarchical tensors, or related low-rank factorizations.
[1-10:P244; UTF16 15528] When interactions are structured and ranks remain low, a function defined over billions of combinations can be stored and queried through small tensor cores. Tensor-network methods can avoid dense storage of high-dimensional arrays in favorable cases. arXiv
[1-10:P246; UTF16 15800] Catalog discontinuities, rare failure states, topology changes, contact, and highly coupled constraints can cause rank explosion. Approximation error may also be concentrated exactly in safety-critical corners.
[1-10:P248; UTF16 16019] Use tensor compression for local response tables, uncertainty maps, and repeated operators—never as the sole authoritative representation.
[1-10:P252; UTF16 16161] 4.10 Adversarial theorem game
[1-10:P254; UTF16 16199] Alternate between:
[1-10:P255; UTF16 16218] a synthesizer proposing the least complicated realization family satisfying current obligations;
[1-10:P256; UTF16 16315] an adversary finding the smallest plausible counterexample that invalidates a claim.
[1-10:P257; UTF16 16400] The design acquires complexity only when failure search proves that complexity necessary.
[1-10:P259; UTF16 16509] It avoids adding redundancy, detail, and conservative complexity without evidence. Minimal counterexamples expose deep weaknesses more efficiently than random simulation.
[1-10:P261; UTF16 16694] The game may converge to a merely feasible artifact rather than an excellent one. It can cycle, overfit to known adversaries, and struggle with incomparable human values.
[1-10:P263; UTF16 16873] Use adversarial counterexamples as the principal refinement mechanism, but retain decision coverage and tradeoff reasoning.
[1-10:P267; UTF16 17000] 4.11 Rational computation market
[1-10:P269; UTF16 17041] Treat every possible analysis, proof, split, simulation, or human query as an action with an expected value of computation.
[1-10:P271; UTF16 17184] The system stops spending hours on analyses that cannot change the decision and prioritizes cheap calculations capable of killing huge branches. Rational metareasoning formalizes this general idea. ar5iv
[1-10:P273; UTF16 17402] Metareasoning does not change the underlying representation. An exquisitely scheduled brute-force optimizer is still brute force. Estimating the value of every computation can itself consume the budget.

[1-10:P279; UTF16 17712] 4.12 Universal differentiable engineering IR
[1-10:P281; UTF16 17765] Compile continuous, discrete, symbolic, and topological decisions into relaxed differentiable forms. Use gradients throughout.
[1-10:P283; UTF16 17911] Gradients can move thousands of continuous variables using relatively few physics solves.
