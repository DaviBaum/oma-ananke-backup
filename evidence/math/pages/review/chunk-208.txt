[21-30:P32638; UTF16 1080199] ADD-SOS28.1 — Experiment-Family Refinement Theorem
[21-30:P32640; UTF16 1080255] \[ \mathcal F_1\subseteq\mathcal F_2. \]
[21-30:P32642; UTF16 1080304] \[ x\equiv_{\mathcal F_k}y \iff \forall f\in\mathcal F_k,\quad f(x)=f(y). \]
[21-30:P32644; UTF16 1080387] \[ \boxed{ x\equiv_{\mathcal F_2}y \Longrightarrow x\equiv_{\mathcal F_1}y. } \]
[21-30:P32646; UTF16 1080474] If \(x\equiv_{\mathcal F_2}y\), every computation in \(\mathcal F_2\) agrees on x,y. Since every computation in \(\mathcal F_1\) also lies in \(\mathcal F_2\), every computation in \(\mathcal F_1\) agrees. Hence:
[21-30:P32647; UTF16 1080687] \[ x\equiv_{\mathcal F_1}y. \]
[21-30:P32649; UTF16 1080720] Therefore an expanded experiment family can split old quotient classes but cannot merge classes that were already distinguished:
[21-30:P32650; UTF16 1080849] \[ \boxed{ \mathcal F_1\subseteq\mathcal F_2 \Longrightarrow Q_{\mathcal F_2} \text{ refines } Q_{\mathcal F_1}. } \]
