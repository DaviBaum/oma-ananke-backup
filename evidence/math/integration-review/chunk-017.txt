[19231] C. Risk
[19232] THM-DYN22 — CVaR variational representation
[19233] For integrable loss \(Z\) and \(0<\alpha<1\):
[19234] \[ \operatorname{CVaR}_\alpha(Z) = \min_{\eta} \left[ \eta+\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\mathbb E[(Z-\eta)_+] \right]. \]
[19235] Proof. For a finite distribution, the objective is convex and piecewise linear. Its subgradient is:
[19236] \[ 1-\frac{P(Z>\eta)}{1-\alpha} -\frac{\theta P(Z=\eta)}{1-\alpha}, \quad \theta\in[0,1]. \]
[19237] Zero belongs to the subgradient exactly at an \(\alpha\)-quantile. Evaluating the objective there yields the mean of the upper tail with the appropriate fractional mass at the quantile. Approximation by simple variables and \(L^1\) continuity extends the identity to integrable losses. \(\square\)
[19238] THM-DYN23 — CVaR monotonicity
[19239] If \(X\le Y\) almost surely, then:
[19240] \[ \operatorname{CVaR}_\alpha(X) \le \operatorname{CVaR}_\alpha(Y). \]
[19241] Proof. For every \(\eta\),
[19242] \[ (X-\eta)_+\le(Y-\eta)_+. \]
[19243] Compare objectives and take infima. \(\square\)
[19244] THM-DYN24 — CVaR translation equivariance
[19245] For constant \(c\):
[19246] \[ \operatorname{CVaR}_\alpha(Z+c) = \operatorname{CVaR}_\alpha(Z)+c. \]
[19247] Proof. Substitute \(\eta'=\eta-c\) in DEF-DYN43. \(\square\)
[19248] THM-DYN25 — Upper-tail CVaR dominates expectation under the standard loss convention
[19249] For integrable loss \(Z\) and \(\alpha\in[0,1)\):
[19250] \[ \operatorname{CVaR}_\alpha(Z)\ge\mathbb E[Z]. \]
[19251] Proof. The dual representation is:
[19252] \[ \operatorname{CVaR}_\alpha(Z) = \sup_{\substack{\xi\ge0\\ \mathbb E\xi=1\\ \xi\le(1-\alpha)^{-1}}} \mathbb E[\xi Z]. \]
[19253] The density \(\xi=1\) is feasible, so the supremum is at least \(\mathbb E[Z]\). The statement concerns upper-tail loss CVaR; other sign conventions must be transformed explicitly. \(\square\)
