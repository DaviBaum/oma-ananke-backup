[571] 7. Central representation decision
[572] 7.1 Rejected alternatives
[573] A single scene graph is rejected because scene hierarchy does not encode net incidence, unit semantics, proof obligations, or recertification dependencies.
[574] A single ordinary directed multigraph is rejected because a graph edge cannot simultaneously and unambiguously mean:
[575] cell adjacency;
[576] a physical connection;
[577] a control dependency;
[578] a proof dependency;
[579] a rewrite correspondence;
[580] a route segment;
[581] a constraint incidence.
[582] A single hypergraph is insufficient because exact geometry and cellular boundary operators are not naturally reduced to hyperedges without losing geometric semantics.
[583] A factor graph is insufficient because factors describe constraint scope but do not own geometry or system identity.
[584] A single category or operad is rejected as the concrete storage representation. Category-theoretic structure is valuable for composition, but serialization and checking require explicit finite carriers, relations, maps, and versions.

[1131] LEM-SIR1 — Finite compatibility relation
[1132] For finite component carriers, the set of compatible tuples satisfying a finite family of identity-equality predicates is finite.
[1133] Proof. A finite product of finite sets is finite. The compatible tuples form a subset of that product. Every subset of a finite set is finite. ∎
[1134] LEM-SIR2 — Pullback projection agreement
[1135] If \(x=(x_c)_c\) belongs to the SIR pullback, then every pair of shared references has equal image in \(\mathfrak I\).
[1136] Proof. This equality is exactly the defining compatibility condition of the pullback. ∎
[1137] LEM-SIR3 — Scalar quantization bound
[1138] For nearest-lattice rounding,
[1139] \[ |x-Q_\delta(x)|\le\frac{\delta}{2}. \]
[1140] Proof. Every real number lies within half the spacing of at least one nearest lattice point. The deterministic tie rule chooses one of the two equidistant points at exact half spacing. ∎
[1141] LEM-SIR4 — Hausdorff perturbation inequality
[1142] If \(d_H(A,A')\le\varepsilon_A\) and \(d_H(B,B')\le\varepsilon_B\), then
[1143] \[ |d(A,B)-d(A',B')| \le \varepsilon_A+\varepsilon_B. \]
[1144] Proof. Choose \(a'\in A'\), \(b'\in B'\) arbitrarily close to minimizers. Select \(a\in A\), \(b\in B\) within the respective Hausdorff bounds. The triangle inequality gives
[1145] \[ \|a-b\| \le \|a-a'\|+\|a'-b'\|+\|b'-b\|. \]
[1146] Taking infima gives one direction; interchange primed and unprimed sets for the other. ∎
[1147] LEM-SIR5 — Transform composition associativity
[1148] Composable affine transforms satisfy
[1149] \[ (T_{kl}\circ T_{jk})\circ T_{ij} = T_{kl}\circ(T_{jk}\circ T_{ij}). \]
[1150] Proof. Function composition is associative. For affine matrices, direct multiplication gives the same result by associativity of matrix multiplication and distributivity over translation. ∎
[1151] LEM-SIR6 — Translational Minkowski membership
[1152] For sets \(B,O\subseteq\mathbb R^3\),
[1153] \[ (x+B)\cap O\neq\varnothing \iff x\in O\oplus(-B). \]
[1154] Proof. The left side holds iff there exist \(b\in B,o\in O\) with \(x+b=o\), equivalently \(x=o-b\in O\oplus(-B)\). The reverse follows by reversing the algebra. ∎
[1155] LEM-SIR7 — Incidence cancellation
[1156] For a directed net in which every internal edge has one \(+1\) and one \(-1\) incidence,
[1157] \[ \mathbf 1^\top B=0. \]
[1158] Proof. Each column sum is \(+1-1=0\). ∎
[1159] LEM-SIR8 — Running-intersection propagation

[1161] Proof. This is the running-intersection hypothesis applied to the connected subtree of bags containing the variable. ∎
